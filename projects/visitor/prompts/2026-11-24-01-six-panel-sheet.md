```text
Redraw the storyboard for my 18-second vertical short "A thought is a visitor".
Use my attached 4-panel image for the story only. Change the house and the staging as described below.

OUTPUT
One contact sheet: 6 panels, 3 columns × 2 rows, each panel vertical 9:16, separated by thin dark grey lines. No captions, numbers or text inside the panels.

STYLE
- Pure black background #000000. White lines #ffffff. One accent: orange #ff6a00.
- No other colours, gradients, grey fills, textures, sketchy strokes or 3D perspective.
- Thin, precise, smooth, unbroken lines of equal width. No smoke, no chimney, no comic-style knock marks.

THE HOUSE (same position and size in every panel)
- Strict front view, simple gabled silhouette, wide, slightly below the vertical centre, leaving black space above.
- The walls and roof are 16–20 nested parallel white contours, like ripples shaped like a house, tightly spaced. The inside is an empty black room.
- On the right side there is one closed door, seen edge-on: a single vertical white line with a small gap at the bottom.
- One continuous horizontal floor line runs through the room, under the door and out onto the porch on the right. Leave room on the porch for a visitor.

THE CHARACTERS
- The soul: a small solid orange circle with a soft glow, inside the house. No face, no limbs.
- The key: a simple thin orange line key, made from the soul's own light.
- Each visitor is ONE continuous white line: a spring, a wheel, a tangled ball. No faces.
- Visitors stand outside on the porch, smaller than the house.

PANELS
1. THE KNOCK. The contours are calm and straight. The spring is on the porch. A ripple starts where it touches the door and travels through the nested contours. The soul rests on the floor, bright.
2. THE SPRING RULES. The spring holds the orange key. All house contours are compressed into a springy zigzag-wave rhythm copied from the spring. The soul, visibly dimmer and smaller, is tossed near the ceiling.
3. THE WHEEL RULES. The wheel holds the key. All contours have become nearly round, like a wheel. The floor inside is curved and tilted; the dim soul is sliding.
4. THE PAUSE. A tight tangled ball is at the door. The house is calm again. The soul is at the door; its key is stopped exactly halfway through the gap under the door. Complete stillness, tension.
5. THE CHOICE. The key has been pulled back and lies beside the bright soul. Outside, the porch line ripples under the tangled ball. Inside, the floor is perfectly flat for the first time. House contours are calm.
6. THE VISITOR. The soul sits right against the inside of the closed door, bright and warm, its key beside it. The ball, looser and softer now, rests against the outside. They share the same floor line, only the door between them. Leave the upper third empty for a title.

IMPORTANT
- The house must remain recognisable in panels 2 and 3, as the same contours bent by the visitor's rhythm.
- The last panel must feel like quiet company. No line may connect the ball to the soul.
```
