# A thought is a visitor — brief

## Core statement

**A thought can come and knock, but it rules the house only when you hand it the key.**

A thought need not be chased away, and it need not be obeyed. Emotion: funny, then tense, then quiet peace. The ending must read as hospitality without control, never as rejection, so it is the soul itself that rolls to the door.

## Story

| Image | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| House | The inner world. |
| Orange soul, no face | Its inhabitant. The bundle of thin orange lines is its body and makes its gestures: bed, arm and hand, roots, hammock. |
| White freehand scribble | A thought: one unbroken line with a personality. Never geometric, never mechanical. |
| Key | Attention and control. Thin, orange, made of the soul's own light: giving it away leaves the soul smaller and dimmer. |

Three thoughts come to one house. The first two are given the key automatically and the house takes on their nature: it bounces for the spring, rolls for the wheel. At the third knock the soul stops with the key halfway under the door, keeps it, and goes to sit by the door with the guest still on the porch.

## Beats (18 s)

| Time | Frame | Beat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 s | 1 | **The knock.** A springy, curious scribble touches the door. The soul dozes on orange waves. |
| | 2 | **Giving away the key.** Automatically, a hand of lines pushes the key fully under the door; the thought's thin white tendril takes it. |
| 3–6.5 s | 3 | **Spring rules.** The house bends and bounces, loose topographic waves outside; the tiny dim soul is tossed to the ceiling on a stretched ribbon of lines rooted in the floor. Then the spring gets bored, throws the key back and leaves; the house straightens. |
| 6.5–9 s | 4 | **Wheel rules**, twice as fast. The house tries to roll clockwise like a bowl; the soul slides up the right wall, its lines swept behind. |
| 9–13.5 s | 5 | **The pause.** A dense anxious tangle knocks three times. The key stops exactly halfway under the door. Half a second of silence, then one very gentle knock. |
| | 6 | **The choice.** The arm curls back holding the key close, the soul is bright. Strong waves only outdoors, stopping at the house. Inside, the lines are flat and the house is straight. |
| 13.5–18 s | 7 | **The visitor.** The soul rests against the inside of the door, cradled in its waves; the key lies apart on the floor. The same tangle, relaxed into 7–8 soft loops, leans on the outside. Nothing connects them but the shared ground line. Phrase in the empty upper third: "A thought is a visitor." |

## Art direction

Follows [`STYLE.md`](../../STYLE.md). Specific to this piece:

- **House:** as in frame 1: simple gable, narrow left side wall and roof plane for depth, open front. The closed door is the foreshortened right wall, with a small handle and a real gap beneath. No steps, chimney, smoke or comic knock marks.
- **Ground:** one line for the floor and the porch.
- **Line weight:** house and waves thin, the thought slightly thicker (frame 1 proportions). Frame 4's thought in the reference is too thick.
- **Waves:** organic, uneven, topographic; outside the house only; never concentric or evenly spaced. Spring waves bounce, wheel waves sweep clockwise. Knock ripples are uneven arcs from the touch point, not a Wi-Fi icon.
- **Key:** teeth in the keyhole, ring at the thought.
- **The tangle in frames 5 and 7 is the same line:** 7 is a relaxation of 5.

Exceptions and proposals:

- *Proposed:* the phrase's noun "visitor" is orange (`STYLE.md` says verbs).
- The house is drawn with single strokes, not a family of 20–30 contours. The families are the soul's lines and the waves.
